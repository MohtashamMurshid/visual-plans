The thesis

Evaluate the product through a declared population, not one average prompt. MatrAIx joins a queryable persona population, four interaction environments, versioned task contracts, shared telemetry, and subgroup reporting into one simulated-user loop.

The result that changes decisions.

Same cohort · different persona model

First-order factor

The simulator backbone can reverse the product verdict.

On Notion plan choice, paid selection ranged from 23.2% with Opus 4.8 to 93.9% with Haiku 4.5. The paper requires reporting the persona model and checking important findings across models.

OpenBB

Trust was the strongest stable subgroup signal. All three models ordered four trust groups identically; Cramér’s V = 0.228–0.363, all q < 10⁻⁸.

22 pairs

Cross-model consistency was mixed. 14 of 22 defined pair-by-task comparisons pointed in the same direction; median Spearman ρ was +0.29.

88 fields

Model outputs were not interchangeable. Median Cohen’s κ across joinable persona fields was approximately zero for every model pair.

The evidence supports pre-deployment screening, stress testing, subgroup analysis, and version comparison. It does not establish that simulated cohorts estimate real-population behaviour.

What is not proven.

The boundary matters

01 · Representation

8.3B ≠ the world

The public artifact is a 999,847-record coreset. Neither synthetic nor human-grounded cohorts are probability samples of real populations.

02 · Human validity

Adherence ≠ realism

A model can express a declared style without withholding context, pushing back, correcting, or abandoning like a person.

03 · Backbone bias

Self-preference is unisolated

The experiments do not fully cross persona model with system model, so shared-backbone preference cannot be separated from model variation.

04 · Consequence

Hypothesis-generating only

Do not impersonate people, attribute simulated views to communities, or use this as evidence for high-stakes population decisions.

Engineer’s take.

Three durable lessons

01

The useful primitive is the cohort manifest. Reproducible filters, strata, model, seed, task version, and selected IDs matter more than the raw persona count.

02

Never publish one simulator number. Show model arms, subgroup denominators, uncertainty, traces, and integrity exceptions together.

03

Use simulation to find where to look. Let persona agents surface friction cheaply; let matched human evidence decide whether it is real.

Recommended next experiment.

Highest-value missing evidence

Crossed study

Cross persona backbone × system backbone on one task, then human-anchor the disagreements.

Use one SEA-relevant chatbot recovery or subscription-trust task, one frozen cohort, at least two persona models, and two systems under test. Add a small human cohort and compare not only final choices but correction, pushback, withheld context, and abandonment.
